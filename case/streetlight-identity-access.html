Case 01 · StreetLight Data, a Jacobs company · Shipped

Rebuilding Identity & Access for a 5,000-user transportation platform

I owned the Identity & Access roadmap for StreetLight's B2B platform. Redesigned onboarding, self-serve provisioning, role-based access and a single sign-on front door took active adoption up 3.5× and support dependency down 45%.

RoleProduct Manager, Transportation Platform
TeamScrum team of 8 engineers, 2 designers
Scale5,000+ customer users
TimelineDec 2024 – Jul 2026

Product strategy breakdown

01

Context

StreetLight sells a suite of transportation analytics products to agencies and planners: Planning, with multiple dashboards, and Operations, which integrates with Esri ArcGIS. Identity & Access sits under all of them — every user's first and most repeated experience of the platform.

02

Problem

Users signed up but didn't stay active. Onboarding wasn't turning new accounts into regular users.

Access ran through support. Provisioning new packages, changing roles and updating user details depended on support tickets.

Every product had its own login. Customers who bought several products juggled separate credentials and had no single view of what they owned.

03

Who it's for

Customer end users

Planners and operations staff who need to get in and get to the data.

Customer admins

Manage who has access to which products and roles — ideally without filing a ticket.

Internal support & CS

Absorb the cost of every manual access request.

04

Strategic bet

Treat identity and access as a product surface, not plumbing. Make it self-serve and give the whole suite one front door.

The same changes serve two goals at once: cutting support load and growing adoption. A login page that lists every product a customer has purchased also makes the suite easier to discover.

05

What shipped & how I prioritised

Shipped
  • Redesigned onboarding flows
  • New Package Provisioning (self-serve)
  • User Access Redesign — role-based access control
  • User Profile & Preferences
  • Single sign-on across the suite, including the Esri ArcGIS-integrated Operations app
How I chose
  • Support-ticket analysis picked what to make self-serve first
  • Root-cause analysis of platform defects decided what to fix first
  • Sprint and quarterly priorities weighed roadmap bets against technical debt
06

How it works

Sign in onceOne SSO login page for the whole suite
See what you ownEvery purchased product listed in one place
Manage yourselfAdmins provision packages and assign roles without support
IterateHeap behaviour data and experiments tune onboarding
07

Results

3.5×Active user adoption in 8 months (+250%)
−45%Support dependency after self-serve provisioning
−75%Go-to-market workflow turnaround, with Product Marketing

SSO also reduced access-related support tickets and raised multi-product adoption. Live demos, onboarding training and product documentation shortened time-to-value across client segments.

08

Risks & trade-offs

One door is one point of failure. If the SSO page is down, every product is down.

Self-serve has to be safe. Role-based access lets admins move fast, but a wrong role exposes data — permissions need to be clear by design.

New features vs technical debt. Every sprint traded roadmap bets against defect fixes; root-cause analysis made that trade explicit.

09

Learning

The support queue is one of the best roadmap inputs a platform PM has. Every repeated ticket is a feature waiting to be made self-serve.
